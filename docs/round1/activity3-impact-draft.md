**Who benefits, and roughly how many:**
First, wholesale shop owners in Khaderpet, Tirupur's largest garment wholesale market, where a 2017 Business Standard report counted over 1,200 units. My relative's shop alone takes 25 to 30 voice orders every two weeks, each worth ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 and written by hand, so a single mistake is costly. Second, the staff who pack from handwritten notes. Third, the retailers across Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka who buy from these shops and need the right goods and a clear bill in their own language.

**What changes compared with today:**
Today each order of 10 to 15 lines is written up to three times: in the notebook, on the GST bill and in the khata. Because each order is so large, one wrong rate or missed line can cost thousands of rupees or a buyer's trust. With Order Kural, the owner speaks or forwards the order once, in Tamil, Malayalam, Kannada or Hindi. Wrong items, sizes and rates are caught at the read-back, before packing, not after dispatch. The GST bill reaches the buyer on WhatsApp in a minute instead of being copied at day end. Credit is recorded automatically and reminders go out on time, so less money stays unpaid. The owner changes nothing about how buyers order.

**How you'd know it worked:**
A one-month pilot at one Khaderpet shop (about 50 to 60 orders), compared with the previous month of notebook orders. Targets:
1. time from end of call to bill sent: under 3 minutes per order;
2. order mistakes found after packing or reported by buyers: down by half;
3. credit older than 30 days: lower at the end of the pilot than at the start;
4. share of orders the owner enters through Order Kural by choice: over 70% in the second fortnight.
The strongest sign: the owner keeps using it after the pilot without being asked.

**The main risk:**
Speech recognition may mishear orders: mixed languages, garment trade words, size codes and shop noise. A wrong quantity or rate becomes a wrong bill. The read-back step and the owner's spoken "yes" before any bill is sent reduce this, but if corrections take longer than writing in the notebook, the owner will stop using it. The pilot will measure correction rate per order to test this honestly.
