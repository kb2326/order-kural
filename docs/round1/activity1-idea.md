The problem:
In Khaderpet, Tirupur's garment wholesale market, small wholesalers selling women's and kids' wear (co-ord sets, palazzos, leggings, crop tops, shorts) receive almost every order as a phone call or a WhatsApp voice note. While the buyer is still talking, the owner writes the order by hand in a notebook: item, quantity, rate, total. A typical order has 10 to 15 lines and is worth on a average ₹20,000 to ₹30,000. The same order is then written a second time as a GST bill, and a third time in a khata book if it is on credit. Key details, such as the buyer's name, the size split, the transport service and the payment terms, often stay only in the voice note or in the owner's memory. The result is missed items, wrong rates, corrected totals, wrong dispatches and unpaid credit that nobody follows up.

Who has it, where and how often:
Wholesale garment shop owners and their staff in Khaderpet, Tirupur, and the retailers across Tamil Nadu and Kerala who buy from them. A single small shop handles 25 to 30 voice orders every two weeks, in Tamil, Malayalam and Hindi. Each order is copied by hand up to three times, so the errors repeat with every order.

The voice-first product:
Order Kural ("kural" means voice in Tamil) is a voice ordering assistant that works inside WhatsApp.
1. The owner forwards the buyer's voice note, or speaks the order as a short voice message right after the call, in Tamil, Malayalam or Hindi: the buyer's name, the items, quantities, sizes and transport, and whether it is on credit.
2. Order Kural turns the speech into a structured order, fills in rates from the owner's price list, applies minimum quantities per size, and reads the order and total back aloud for confirmation.
3. The owner says "make the GST bill" , and it creates the bill and sends it to the buyer on WhatsApp.
4. Credit orders are recorded in a digital khata automatically. At month end, each buyer gets a payment reminder in their own language.

Why voice comes first:
Voice is not a feature added to this product; it is the only way the trade already works. Buyers place orders by speaking, in three different languages, and the owner takes them with the phone in one hand and stock in the other. Typing a 15-line order with sizes into an app is slower than writing it in the notebook, so a typing-based tool would never replace it. Only speech is fast enough to capture the order at the moment it happens, in the language it is spoken. Order Kural removes the copying, keeps every detail in one record, and asks the owner to change nothing about how he works.
